# composer-2.5 — Design Rationale

Five distinct homepage directions for Naman Sharma's Product/UX portfolio. Each is a self-contained long-scroll page built from `resume.md` and `portfolio-content.md`.

---

## d1 — Jaipur Editorial

**Concept:** Architectural editorial — Jaipur craft and haveli geometry translated into a portfolio for design leads and hiring managers.

**Key decisions:**
- Palette: midnight indigo, peacock teal, copper (Jaipur pigments — deliberately not cream/terracotta)
- Type: Cormorant Garamond display + Source Sans 3 body
- Asymmetric case-study spreads with alternating visual/content rhythm
- CSS jali lattice dividers as the signature element between sections

**Trade-offs:** Rich and authoritative, but the editorial pace is slower than metric-forward directions. Copper accent used sparingly to avoid ornamental overload.

---

## d2 — Field Notes

**Concept:** Research notebook — the page reads like a UX researcher's working document, foregrounding methodology and findings over visual polish.

**Key decisions:**
- Lined-paper background with red margin annotations (signature)
- Newsreader serif + IBM Plex Mono for stamps, transcripts, and metrics
- Sticky-note insight board, interview transcript blocks, study cards with dashed wireframe placeholders
- Wikipedia KaiOS study given equal visual weight to product case studies

**Trade-offs:** Highly distinctive for a research-led designer, but the monospace density may feel academic to recruiters scanning quickly. Optimized for peers and design leads who value rigor.

---

## d3 — Ship Fast

**Concept:** Builder energy — dark, metric-forward, startup-minded. For founders and product teams who care about outcomes first.

**Key decisions:**
- Near-black void + flame orange accent (not acid green — builder/tool aesthetic)
- Syne display + DM Sans; bento grid as signature layout
- Animated metric counters on scroll (respects `prefers-reduced-motion`)
- Real numbers front and center: 20%, 60%, $500K, 4.8★

**Trade-offs:** High impact and scannable, but the dark bento aesthetic risks feeling "startup template" if the orange weren't tied to shipping/building language. Motion is limited to one orchestrated moment.

---

## d4 — No Synergy Zone

**Concept:** Anti-jargon manifesto — turns Naman's own words about hating corporate design speak into the visual thesis. Brutalist typography as content strategy.

**Key decisions:**
- Pure black/white + signal red; Archivo Black + Work Sans
- Buzzword wall with struck-through corporate phrases (signature)
- Zero border-radius, heavy rules, uppercase structure
- Hero headline literally crosses out synergy language

**Trade-offs:** Bold and memorable, polarizing by design. Works if the reviewer shares Naman's anti-jargon stance; may feel aggressive to conservative corporate hiring. Highest aesthetic risk of the five.

---

## d5 — Quiet Compound

**Concept:** Slow craft — "consistency compounds" as the visual and narrative spine. For reviewers who value depth over flash.

**Key decisions:**
- Sand, slate, dusty rose palette; Lora + Karla
- Chapter-based navigation with compound-growth curve SVG (signature)
- Narrow reading column (~680px), generous whitespace
- Principles section leads with Naman's own consistency quote

**Trade-offs:** Calm and credible, but the restrained palette and narrow column may not stop a fast-scrolling recruiter. Best for design-literate audiences who read, not skim.
