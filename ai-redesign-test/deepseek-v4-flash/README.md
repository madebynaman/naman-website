# DeepSeek V4 Flash — 5 Design Directions

## d1 — The Quiet Authority (Editorial / Minimal)

**Concept:** A restrained, literary-adjacent portfolio that projects confidence through restraint. Think broadsheet design meets personal monograph — the content does the work, the design steps back.

**Key decisions:**
- Literata (serif) for headings + Inter (sans) for body — a serious-but-warm editorial pairing
- Narrow article-body width (680px) for the narrative sections; wider container for work grid
- Gold/brass accent (#C4A882, #8B7355) on near-black text against warm off-white (#F5F0EB) — quiet luxury palette
- Pull quotes with ornate drop caps as structural breathing room
- Hairline rules as the only decorative element
- Stats presented as a simple three-column border-top group — assertive without shouting

**Trade-offs:** The restrained palette risks blending in if the typography isn't executed with precision. Low energy — this direction assumes the reader arrives ready to engage. Not for skimmers.

## d2 — The Anti-Jargon (Brutalist / Confident)

**Concept:** A direct response to Naman's own "I hate jargon" manifesto. Brutalist in spirit, not in laziness — every element is intentionally raw. The page opens with the manifesto itself: a strikethrough of every design buzzword.

**Key decisions:**
- Black background (#0C0C0C) with chartreuse accent (#D0FF00) — confrontational, high-stakes
- Space Grotesk (headings) + JetBrains Mono (data/labels) — technical, precise, unpretentious
- "I do not foster innovation through synergizing..." as the hero hook — the portfolio's thesis as a strikethrough
- Grid-based work list with numbered entries and metric columns — factual, no imagery to soften the message
- Yellow CTA section — single interaction point, no friction

**Trade-offs:** Polarizing by design. Will alienate viewers who prefer softer brand experiences. The high-contrast scheme is striking but fatiguing on long scrolls. Works best for hiring managers who are tired of portfolios that sound the same.

## d3 — The Craftsman (Warm / Studio)

**Concept:** A warm, workshop-like portfolio that emphasizes process and craft. Feels like walking into a well-lit studio where thoughtful work happens. Builds trust through transparency about how things are made.

**Key decisions:**
- Warm cream (#F8F4ED) base with DM Serif Display + DM Sans — approachable, humanist
- Rounded cards with subtle shadows for work items — tactile, inviting
- Ochre/gold accent (#D4A373, #B88A5C) — warm without being sweet
- Process presented as a numbered timeline with serif numerals — emphasizes the method
- Principles shown as individual cards (not bullets) — gives each maxim room to breathe
- Contact panel in dark brown with warm accent — intimate, like a studio conversation

**Trade-offs:** The warm palette is familiar territory for designer portfolios; risks feeling less distinctive. Card-based layouts are comfortable but not pushing formal boundaries. Process timeline is thorough but long.

## d4 — The Systems Thinker (Modern / Tech)

**Concept:** A structured, systematic portfolio that foregrounds Naman's computer engineering background. Designed like a design-system documentation site — organized, hierarchical, data-driven. Makes the case that good design is repeatable and measurable.

**Key decisions:**
- Clean white/gray palette (#F8FAFC) with blue accent (#3B82F6) — technical, clinical, confident
- Inter for everything, DM Mono for data — efficient, no-nonsense typography
- Hero sidebar with structured metadata (location, experience, education, approach) — like a system component spec
- Metrics presented as a border-separated grid — clinical comparison of outcomes
- Process shown as a connected node diagram — the loop, not a linear timeline
- Contact as a two-column grid — email + links, efficient

**Trade-offs:** Can read as cold or impersonal — this direction prioritizes credibility over warmth. The blue/white palette is common in tech. Risks under-representing the creative side of Naman's work.

## d5 — Curious Generalist (Playful / Experimental)

**Concept:** A vibrant, personality-forward portfolio that celebrates breadth. Naman's "ask me about" interests (indie dog Jerry, hip-hop, finance, games) aren't footnotes — they're a design feature. Irregular layouts, gradient flourishes, and playful hierarchy signal a designer who doesn't color inside the lines.

**Key decisions:**
- Purple-to-pink gradient hero (#7C3AED → #D946EF) with cyan accents — energetic, unconventional
- Playfair Display (serif) + Space Grotesk (sans) — expressive without being unserious
- Rotated floating "impact cards" as the hero visual — plays with convention
- "Ask Me About" section as a full-width dark panel with emoji-labeled interest cards — the signature element
- Gradient stat tiles, process cards with colored top bars, split work-piece layout — variety without chaos
- Contact as two approachable boxes (send a note / find me online) — low friction

**Trade-offs:** The purple/pink gradient risks being trendy. The playfulness may read as less serious for enterprise/hiring-manager audiences. More visual complexity means longer load time perception (even without external assets). The "generalist" framing is accurate but could be mistaken for lack of depth.
