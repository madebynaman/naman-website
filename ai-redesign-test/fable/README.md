# Fable — 5 Directions

Five bets on the same brief: one long-scroll homepage for Naman Sharma,
Product/UX designer. Each direction takes a different true thing about him and
builds the whole visual system around it.

Process note: the mandated `frontend-design` skill is not installed in this
environment; the closest available design skill (`artifact-design`) was loaded
and followed before any design work.

---

## d1 — The Dossier

**Concept.** Naman's differentiator is research — field interviews in three
cities, hypothesis lists, usability testing. So the homepage *is* a research
dossier about him: sticky index, numbered sections (§01–§08), case studies as
Exhibits A–D, findings cross-referenced, an "off the record" appendix.

**Key decisions.** Spectral (serif) + IBM Plex Mono + Caveat pen annotations;
ruled-paper ground with archival stamp red and annotation blue; NDA-redaction
bars double as the placeholder-image language (his portfolio is literally an
NDA portfolio, so redactions are honest, not decorative); wireframe-style
exhibit plates label exactly what image goes where.

**Trade-offs.** The conceit demands reading; it rewards a design-literate
audience (hiring managers, leads) and asks more of a skimming recruiter. The
sticky index mitigates this by making the whole page scannable in one glance.

## d2 — No Jargon

**Concept.** His intro literally lists the jargon he refuses to use. This
direction turns that into the thesis: a brutalist typographic manifesto that
opens with "I design products users love, for brands that care. That's it."
and then strikes through the entire jargon graveyard before showing receipts.

**Key decisions.** Archivo Black at poster scale + Space Mono; white ground,
2px black borders everywhere, hyperlink-blue (#0000EE) as the only color plus
a marker-yellow highlight; work as full-width ledger rows that invert on
hover; placeholder images as hard-edged crossed-out frames (the brutalist
native idiom for "image goes here"); stats marquee as moving proof.

**Trade-offs.** Loud and confrontational — polarizing by design. If the
reviewer wants warmth, this isn't it; the bet is that bluntness is the most
memorable possible packaging for a person whose whole voice is anti-fluff.

## d3 — Pink City

**Concept.** Nobody else's portfolio can be from Jaipur. A longform magazine
profile — masthead, cover feature, standfirst, drop cap, pull quote, figures —
written in third person like a design-press profile, with the Pink City's
arches as a recurring motif and palette source.

**Key decisions.** Gloock + Instrument Serif italics + Karla; sandstone
ground, plum ink, Jaipur rose, gold ornaments; case studies as numbered
"Features" with framed gallery plates (Fig. 1–4) as image placeholders;
principles inverted on plum as "The Creed"; roman-numeral method section.
Third-person voice is the risk that makes it feel editorial rather than
self-promotional.

**Trade-offs.** The most text-forward direction — it sells depth and taste
over speed. Third-person copy could read as affected to some readers; the
payoff is that it positions him as someone worth profiling.

## d4 — Control Panel

**Concept.** He "helps clients measure the impact of UX on business" and has
a founder's mindset — so the portfolio reads as a metrics dashboard:
`naman.os`, a designer operations console. KPI tiles are his real outcomes
(+20%, −60%, $500K, 4.8★), projects are rows with status pills, career is an
uptime track, principles are a config file, history is an event log.

**Key decisions.** Sora + Figtree + JetBrains Mono; engineering-grey ground
with white panels and a teal accent (deliberately not the dark-terminal
cliché); inline SVG sparklines drawn as real chart marks; the dashboard
grammar itself proves he can design dense data UI — the format is a work
sample. Placeholder thumbs are dashed image slots labeled per screen.

**Trade-offs.** Metaphor discipline: pushed far enough to be coherent
(config, logs, uptime) but numbers-first framing downplays craft/visual work.
Chosen anyway because the audience that hires him buys outcomes.

## d5 — Liner Notes

**Concept.** "Ask me about: hip-hop music." The portfolio as a vinyl LP —
Side A is the work (four tracks "feat. Asian Paints", with hooks like +20%
and runtimes), Side B is the six-step process, principles are production
credits, career is a discography, clients are shout-outs, contact is booking.

**Key decisions.** Big Shoulders at poster weight + Courier Prime typewriter
liner notes + Libre Franklin; riso two-ink look (orange + electric blue on
cream) with a paper-grain dot texture; a CSS-only spinning record with a
catalog-number label; "Advisory: 100% jargon-free content" sticker ties back
to his actual voice. NDA note handled as "masters held under NDA."

**Trade-offs.** The most personality-forward and least conventional bet — a
conservative hiring manager might read it as a stunt. Every fact in it is
still real and load-bearing (tracks are real projects, credits are his real
principles), so the playfulness sits on substance, not instead of it.

---

**Shared constraints honored across all five:** single long anchor-linked
page; Google Fonts with system fallbacks; no other external requests; no
frameworks; all names, roles, dates and numbers from `resume.md` /
`portfolio-content.md`; placeholder visuals are deliberate, labeled CSS/SVG
constructions — no fake photography, no broken images.
