Ask Naman Sharma what he does and you will not hear about paradigms, ideation, or the confluence of form and function. "I design products that users love, for brands that care," he says. "That's it." The brevity is the point — and it is earned. Behind it sits a Computer Engineering degree from Pune, a self-taught design education, and three years of UX practice layered over years of graphic design.

His route in was research. At Hureo he travelled between Pune, Mumbai and Delhi, sitting across from twenty-five JioPhone owners for the Wikimedia Foundation — watching how people actually read, search and struggle on a smart feature phone. That habit of looking first never left. Whether the client is Asian Paints or a two-person startup, the work starts with the customer, not the canvas.

"Problems are solved with functionality, not aesthetics. But once solved — go the extra mile. Humans remember delight."

A curious generalist by his own admission, he has run field studies and usability tests, designed web apps, mobile apps, logos, packaging and print, built sites without code, and is currently re-learning to write it — so ideas can go from sketch to shipped without waiting for permission. Startups hire him to go from nothing to something; enterprises hire him to make complicated things simple.

He works remotely from Jaipur with an indie dog named Jerry supervising from the floor, and he measures his work the way founders do: in conversions, retention and rounds raised — not dribbble likes.

Boost360: finding why users fled in four hours

Thence · 2021 · Research, Design & Usability Testing

New users uninstalled within four hours; only one in five ever signed up. The Creed

Four beliefs, printed in full and practised daily.

Consistency beats talent

Consistent effort compounds into unimaginable results. Not the most talented designer in the world — the one who improves every day.

Let curiosity guide you

Knowing a little about a lot of things is how scattered information becomes connected dots. Every project is a journey of discovery.

Form follows function

Solve the need first. Then go the extra mile and make it beautiful — because humans remember delight.

Give a damn

A founder's mindset and a high level of agency. Ownership of every project, and the extra mile as standard practice.

The Method

Six movements, in order. No shortcuts, no theatre.

I

Research

Understand the problem, the customer, and the possible solutions before anything is drawn.

II

User Journey

Personas, journeys and decision-making, mapped until the important insights surface.

III

Design Exploration

Go wide. Test, iterate and collaborate through a range of possible solutions.

IV

Design Refinement

Polish the chosen direction, document the decisions, prepare for handoff.

V

Handoff

Work closely with engineering and product so the build matches the design.

VI

Monitoring

Live is not done. Track the metrics that indicate success, and keep improving.
