001 · Abstract

I design products that users love, for brands that care. That's it.

I hate jargon. I don't synergize aesthetics and functionality, orchestrate design revolutions, or perpetuate narratives that transcend boundaries. — from the author's note

Naman Sharma is a self-taught Product / UX designer working remotely from Jaipur, India. Three years in UX on top of a computer-science background, with proven experience making complex products easy to use and helping start-ups build 0 → 1.

The work is rooted in research and constant improvement — improving people's lives with design and tech, one pixel at a time. The portfolio you're reading is a bibliography of that work: each entry is traceable to a real project, a real outcome, and a real trade-off.

What follows isn't a gallery of polished screens. It's an index — the kind a librarian would keep — of problems, methods, and what the work actually achieved in the world.

002 · Standing axioms
α
Axiom I

Consistency beats talent.

Consistent effort toward a craft compounds into results talent can't match on its own. Not the most talented designer in the world — striving for consistency and constant improvement, every day.

β
Axiom II

Let curiosity guide you.

A little about a lot of things, to connect dots between scattered information. Each project is a journey of discovery, learning, and exploration. Curiosity makes work that actually makes a difference.

γ
Axiom III

Form follows function.

Problems are solved with functionality, not aesthetics. But once the problem is solved, going the extra mile on beauty creates delight — and humans remember delight.

δ
Axiom IV

Give a damn.

A founder's mindset, real ownership, and collaboration as a shared responsibility. High agency, and the willingness to go the extra mile to make the work succeed.

003 · Method

A six-stage
method.

The same loop on every entry — research first, refinement last, monitoring after launch. Order carries information, so it's numbered; the numbering isn't decoration.

  1. ResearchUnderstand the problem, consider possible solutions, learn about the customer and the context they're in.discover
  2. User journeyDive deep into the journey to uncover insights. Personas, journeys, and how decisions actually get made.map
  3. Design explorationGo wide. Explore a range of possible solutions. Test, iterate, collaborate, work the problem at hand.diverge
  4. Design refinementRefine the chosen direction and polish the visual design. Prepare for handoff and document the decisions made.converge
  5. HandoffWork closely with engineering and product to ensure a successful handoff and effective implementation.ship
  6. MonitoringA live project isn't a finished project. Monitor performance and keep track of the metrics that indicate success.measure

Asian Paints —
Dealer Portal

The problem

How might we improve the dealer experience — digitizing order placement and supply-chain tracking — while increasing transaction volumes and average order value?

Dealers didn't want to call a salesperson for every order. Every competitor already had a digital portal, and dealers were uniformly frustrated with all of them. The portal needed role-based abstraction for users with varying privileges, and Asian Paints held demand data it could use to nudge dealers intelligently.

What the work did

  • Heuristic evaluation and end-to-end redesign of the existing dealer portal.
  • Intelligent, personalized product recommendations based on each dealer's order history.
  • Periodic and order-size incentives to lift average order value; loyalty points and personalized promotions.
  • Order a product in under 10 seconds by scanning its barcode.

Boost360 —
Onboarding

The problem

New users were uninstalling the app before they even signed up. How might we reduce the uninstall rate and push users to complete onboarding, try the app, and stay?

Signup success rate sat at roughly 20%. Average time to signup was around 30 minutes. The team had eleven hypotheses — from crashes on low-end devices to ad-driven expectation mismatch. Research narrowed it: the signup UX was poor, and there was a real gap between what marketing promised and what the app delivered.

What the work did

  • Made signup visual so the process is easy to understand at a glance.
  • Communicated the app's real USPs during onboarding; built trust with social proof.
  • Created quick wins so the user sees results fast; fixed screen-level UX issues.
  • Aligned marketing communication with reality across every touchpoint.

004.c · NS/WIK/2020

Wikipedia —
KaiOS user research

Agency
Hureo
Year
2020
Role
UX Research · Personas · Journey mapping
With
UX Researchers · Founder
field research 25 participants 3 cities

The study

The Wikimedia Foundation wanted to understand JioPhone users — a smart feature phone running KaiOS — and how they read, searched, and understood Wikipedia on a small keypad screen.

One-on-one, in-person interviews in Pune, Mumbai, and Delhi. 25 participants — students, business owners, homemakers, bank employees, salesmen, field executives — between 18 and 40. Forty-five to sixty minutes each: 30–45 on background, reading and phone habits, then observed task-based usage on their own devices.

What the research surfaced

  • Why JioPhone: smart features at an affordable price, family Jio sims, cheap to repair, durable, familiar keypad, strong battery life.
  • Wishlist: more images and video, English numerals in Hindi articles, an in-article table of contents, in-article search, latest-first ordering, bigger fonts and more colour, a news section.
  • Gap: users didn't know they could search inside Wikipedia, or that Wikipedia existed in regional languages.
