First entry — who & why.

opening note

I design products that users love, for brands that care. That's it.

Self-taught Product / UX designer, three years in UX on top of a computer-science background. Working remotely from Jaipur, India — for start-ups, agencies, and the occasional enterprise that wants UX taken seriously.

The work is rooted in research and constant improvement — improving people's lives with design and tech, one pixel at a time. This notebook is the record: the questions I asked, the people I talked to, what changed when the work shipped, and what didn't.

I hate jargon. I don't foster innovation through synergizing aesthetics and functionality, orchestrate design revolutions, or perpetuate narratives that transcend boundaries. — from the author's note, taped in

02

Four things I keep coming back to.

principles
i.

Consistency beats talent.

Consistent effort compounds into results talent can't match on its own. Not the most talented designer — striving for consistency and constant improvement, every day.

↳ compounding, not heroics
ii.

Let curiosity guide you.

A little about a lot of things, to connect dots between scattered information. Each project is a journey of discovery and exploration. Curiosity makes work that actually makes a difference.

↳ generalist on purpose
iii.

Form follows function.

Problems are solved with functionality, not aesthetics. But once the problem is solved, the extra mile on beauty creates delight — and humans remember delight.

↳ solve first, delight second
iv.

Give a damn.

A founder's mindset, real ownership, collaboration as shared responsibility. High agency, and the willingness to go the extra mile to make the work succeed.

↳ ownership, not attendance

A.

Asian Paints — dealer portal

Thence · 20212021
Role: UX research, UX & UI design With: UI designer · PM · design mgr · client Type: enterprise · supply chain

How might we improve the dealer experience — digitizing order placement and supply-chain tracking — while increasing transaction volumes and AOV?

Dealers didn't want to call a salesperson for every order. Every competitor already had a portal, and dealers were uniformly frustrated with all of them. The portal needed role-based abstraction for users with varying privileges, and Asian Paints held demand data it could use to nudge dealers intelligently.

  • Heuristic evaluation and end-to-end redesign of the existing dealer portal.
  • Intelligent, personalized product recommendations from each dealer's order history.
  • Order-size incentives and loyalty points to lift AOV; data-driven regional promotions.
  • Order a product in under 10 seconds by scanning its barcode.

B.

Boost360 — onboarding

Thence · 20212021
Role: UX research, UX/UI, usability testing With: UI · motion · PM · design mgr · client Type: mobile · funnel fix

New users were uninstalling before they signed up. How might we reduce the uninstall rate and push users to complete onboarding, try the app, and stay?

Signup success sat near 20%. Time to signup averaged ~30 minutes. Eleven hypotheses on the wall — crashes, OTP delays, ad-driven expectation mismatch. Research narrowed it: the signup UX was poor, and marketing had promised something the app didn't quite deliver.

  • Made signup visual so the process reads at a glance.
  • Communicated the app's real USPs during onboarding; built trust with social proof.
  • Created quick wins so users see results fast; fixed screen-level UX issues.
  • Aligned marketing communication with reality across every touchpoint.

C.

Wikipedia — KaiOS user research

Hureo · 20202020
Role: UX research · personas · journey mapping With: UX researchers · founder Type: field research · 25 participants · 3 cities

Understand JioPhone users — a smart feature phone on KaiOS — and how they read, search, and understand Wikipedia on a small keypad screen.

One-on-one, in-person interviews in Pune, Mumbai, and Delhi. 25 participants — students, business owners, homemakers, bank employees, salesmen, field executives, 18–40. Forty-five to sixty minutes each: 30–45 on background and habits, then observed task-based usage on their own devices.

  • Why JioPhone: smart features at an affordable price, family Jio sims, cheap to repair, durable, familiar keypad, strong battery.
  • Wishlist: more images and video, English numerals in Hindi articles, in-article table of contents, in-article search, latest-first ordering, bigger fonts and more colour.
  • Gap: users didn't know they could search inside Wikipedia, or that it existed in regional languages.
