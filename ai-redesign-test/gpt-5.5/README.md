# GPT-5.5 Portfolio Homepage Directions

## d1 - Evidence Board

Concept: an investigative evidence wall for a designer whose work is strongest when it is backed by research, outcomes, and concrete product context. The design uses paper texture, dossier cards, stamps, and gridlines to make case-study evidence feel tangible. The trade-off is that it is intentionally dense and editorial; it favors reviewer scanning over a minimal luxury feel.

## d2 - Product Operations Console

Concept: a product command center for teams that care about operations, conversion, onboarding, and implementation. The layout borrows from internal dashboards and ticket queues, with a sticky side rail and metric-heavy modules. The trade-off is a more utilitarian tone; it is less emotionally warm, but very credible for product and design leads.

## d3 - No-Jargon Poster

Concept: a loud anti-jargon editorial page built around Naman's own plainspoken portfolio voice. The hero rejects vague design language visually, then backs the tone with specific case-study proof. The trade-off is intentional visual aggression; it will be memorable, but may be too sharp for conservative hiring contexts.

## d4 - Field Research Atlas

Concept: a research-first atlas that makes Naman's fieldwork and curiosity the emotional center of the homepage. The map, pins, phone frame, and route language connect the Wikipedia KaiOS study to his broader product-design process. The trade-off is that it foregrounds research more than visual UI craft, so the later sections must carry the product-design breadth.

## d5 - Founder Workshop

Concept: a founder-minded studio page for early teams that need product, UX, web, brand, and no-code support from one high-agency designer. The visual system uses workbench tiles, project bets, and practical process panels to sell range without making it feel scattered. The trade-off is that it is less experimental than the poster or atlas directions, but likely the easiest to expand into a real portfolio.
