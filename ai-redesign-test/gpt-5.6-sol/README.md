# GPT 5.6 Sol — Portfolio directions

## d1 — Evidence dossier

A research-led editorial portfolio built from case files, annotations, and deliberately explicit proof points. Newsreader carries the reflective voice; Manrope and IBM Plex Mono keep the evidence operational. The trade-off is restraint over spectacle: the page feels credible and senior, but intentionally less playful than the other directions.

## d2 — Better route

A transit-map metaphor organizes Naman's work as journeys from problem to impact. The route line, project tickets, stop markers, and high-visibility wayfinding palette make a dense homepage easy to scan. The trade-off is a louder visual system that may feel less appropriate for conservative enterprise audiences.

## d3 — Product control room

A dark product-operations interface frames Naman as a designer who can diagnose systems and partner with technical teams. Console language, module grids, status signals, and deployment-style case studies connect his computer-engineering background to UX practice. The trade-off is a more technical first impression, balanced by a dedicated human layer.

## d4 — Curious practice

A tactile studio direction treats Naman's generalism as the main advantage. Bodoni typography, colored material swatches, rotated working sheets, and collaged placeholders connect product, field research, brand, and web without flattening them into one specialty. The trade-off is looser structure and more visual personality in exchange for memorability.

## d5 — No jargon

A direct typographic manifesto turns Naman's anti-jargon stance into the identity. Oversized condensed type, a functional “jargon shredder,” hard rules, and blunt case-study statements keep attention on clear language and real outcomes. The trade-off is deliberate confrontation: it is the boldest, least neutral direction and will polarize more than the evidence-led options.
