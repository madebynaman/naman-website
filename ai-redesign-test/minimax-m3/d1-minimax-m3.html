01   A small thing, first

I don't do jargon. Ever.

I won't tell you I "foster innovation through synergizing aesthetics and functionality," or that I "navigate the confluence of form and function." I don't orchestrate design revolutions. I don't engineer experiential marvels. I have never, in my life, perpetuated a narrative that transcends boundaries, and I don't plan to start.

The work is the work. Talk straight, ship the thing, measure what happened.

What I will tell you: I take complicated products and make them easy to use. I'm a curious generalist — I've done on-field user research, designed web and mobile apps, drawn logos, built websites, designed print collateral, made social media graphics, and even designed product packaging. 02   Four things I believe

Principles, not platitudes.

I've kept these short. Read them once and you have a working theory of how I'll behave on a project with you.

No. 1 · Effort
A working belief

Consistency beats talent.

I'm not the most talented designer in the room. I am, reliably, in the room. Consistent effort compounds into results that look like talent in hindsight — and I think that's a better bet to make.

No. 2 · Method
A working belief

Let curiosity guide you.

I know a little about a lot of things, and it helps me connect dots between scattered information. Each project is a journey of discovery — and the curiosity is what keeps the work making a difference past the kickoff slide.

No. 3 · Order of ops
A working belief

Form follows function.

Problems are solved with functionality, not aesthetics. At the core of every good product is how it solves for the user's need. Once that's solved, going the extra mile on the visual layer is what creates delight — and humans remember delight.

No. 4 · Stance
A working belief

Give a damn.

I work with a founder's mindset. I take ownership of outcomes, not deliverables. I treat success as a shared responsibility, and I will go the extra mile — because the project is mine too, while it's mine.

03   How I work, in order

The same six steps, every time.

This is a real sequence — the order carries information. Skipping a step costs more time than running it.

  1. Research
    Understanding the problem, considering possible solutions, learning the customer. Talk to five users before drawing anything.
  2. User Journey
    Diving deep into the user journey. Personas, decision moments, the gaps where the current experience loses people.
  3. Design Exploration
    Going wide. A range of possible solutions, tested, iterated, argued over, and then narrowed down together with the team.
  4. Design Refinement
    Picking a direction and polishing it. Visual design, edge cases, handoff documentation, decision records — the unglamorous part.
  5. Handoff
    Working closely with engineering and product so what gets built is what got designed. Smoothing the rough edges between Figma and the codebase.
  6. Monitoring
    Once it's live, the work isn't done. Watching the metrics that tell us whether the redesign actually did the thing it set out to do.

04   Selected work

Three case studies I keep coming back to.

Each is a real problem with a real outcome. The full writeups live elsewhere — these are the ones I'd pitch to a hiring manager first.

2021 · Thence

Asian Paints — Dealer Portal

UX Research · UX Design · UI Design
Figure 01

The problem. Asian Paints dealers were stuck calling sales reps to place orders. Competitor portals existed; dealers hated all of them. Transaction volumes were flat, AOV was flat, and every interaction cost time.

What I did. End-to-end UX audit, then a redesign of twelve screens — home, catalog, product listing, order placement, cart, order tracking, account. Added barcode-scan ordering, personalized recommendations, loyalty points, and order-size incentives to nudge AOV up. Worked alongside a UI designer, PM, design lead, and the client team.

+20% Online transaction volume ↑ User engagement & loyalty
2021 · Thence

Boost360 — Onboarding

UX Research · UX Design · UI Design · Usability Testing
Figure 02

The problem. 80% of new Boost360 users uninstalled the app before signing up. Average time-to-signup was thirty minutes. Something between the marketing and the product was breaking.

What I found. It wasn't crashes, OTP, or ads. It was a mismatch between what marketing promised and what onboarding delivered — a UX problem hiding inside a positioning problem.

What I did. Made signup visual, communicated USPs during onboarding, built trust with social proof, and gave users a quick win before the form fields. Ran usability tests on the new flow until the friction was gone.

−60% Uninstall rate ↓ Time-to-signup
2020 · Hureo / Wikimedia

Wikipedia — KaiOS User Research

UX Research · Personas · User Journey Mapping
Figure 03

The brief. Wikimedia wanted to understand the people using Wikipedia on JioPhones — a smart feature phone on KaiOS. The participants were not smartphone users by choice, and that mattered.

What I did. 25 in-person interviews across Pune, Mumbai, and Delhi. 45–60 minutes each. Recruited, ran the sessions, built personas, mapped journeys, and produced an ecosystem map and a research report.

What came out. A set of recommendations the foundation could actually act on — voice-first interaction, simpler Hindi, video content, search visibility, readability on a tiny screen. The findings shaped how Wikipedia approached KaiOS for the next year.
