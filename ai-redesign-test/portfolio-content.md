## My Process

1. **Research** — Understanding more about the problem, considering possible
   solutions and learning about the customer.
2. **User Journey** — Diving deep into the user journey to uncover important
   insights. Exploring user personas, journeys and understanding decision
   making.
3. **Design Exploration** — Going wide and exploring a range of possible
   solutions. Testing, iterating, collaborating and working through the
   problem at hand.
4. **Design Refinement** — Refining a chosen direction and polishing visual
   design, preparing designs for handoff and documenting decisions made.
5. **Handoff** — Working closely with engineering and product to ensure a
   successful handoff and effective implementation of designs.
6. **Monitoring** — Once a project is live, doesn't mean the work is done.
   It's important to monitor performance and keep a track of metrics that
   indicate success.

## Case Study 1 — Asian Paints: Dealer Portal

- **Agency:** Thence
- **Year:** 2021
- **Role:** UX Research, UX Design, UI Design
- **Collaborated with:** UI Designer, Product Manager, Design Manager, Client Team

### Problem & Goals

**How might we...** improve the user experience for dealers by digitizing order
placement and supply chain tracking while increasing transaction volumes and
AOV.

**What do we want to achieve?**

- Increase conversions
- Increase AOV (Average Order Value)
- Increase overall transaction volumes
- Better, more intuitive user experience

### Research Findings

- Dealers don't want to talk to sales person every time they want to place a
  new order.
- Every competitor in the space has a digital portal that dealers are already
  using.
- Dealers are frustrated and unhappy with the user experience of all digital
  portals.
- Dealers need abstraction for different types of users with varying
  privileges for the digital portal.
- Asian Paints has data about product trends/demand and needs to nudge dealers
  to buy products based on data.

### Proposed Solution

- Heuristic evaluation and end-to-end redesign of the existing dealer portal.
- Introduce new features that will reduce dealer-salesman interaction and
  reduce costs.
- Intelligent, personalized product recommendations based on dealer's previous
  order history.
- Personalized discount promotions and loyalty points based on dealer's
  previous order history.
- Data-driven product promotions based on geographical trends and consumer
  demand.
- Periodic and order-size based incentives to promote an increase in AOV.
- Quickly order products in less than 10 seconds by scanning product barcode.

### Final Designs (screen list)

Home, Trending Products, Categories, Catalog, Product Listing, Place Order,
Stock Check, Wishlist, Cart, Order Tracking, Account Management, Reports

### Business Impact

- **20%** increase in online transaction volume
- Increase in user engagement and loyalty

## Case Study 2 — Boost360: Onboarding

- **Agency:** Thence
- **Year:** 2021
- **Role:** UX Research, UX Design, UI Design, Usability Testing
- **Collaborated with:** UI Designer, Motion Designer, Product Manager, Design
  Manager, Client Team

### Problem & Goals

**What is the problem?**

- New users are uninstalling the app before they signup.
- The app is uninstalled within 4 hrs of app installation.
- Signup success rate is extremely low at 20%.
- Average time for signup success is really high at 30 mins.

**How might we...** reduce the uninstall rate, push the users to complete app
onboarding, try the app, and stick with it.

### Hypotheses (what might be the cause of the high uninstall rate)

- App crashes on low-end devices
- OTP does not arrive on time
- App takes a long time to load
- UX for signup is poor
- Process is difficult to understand
- User is not comfortable in english
- App sends too many notifications
- App asks for too many permissions
- App has too many ads
- Mismatch in real function of the app and marketing communication
- Installs from paid acquisition channels are not properly mapped
- Expectation mismatch in terms of free and paid features

### Research — what was the real reason?

- UX for signup is poor and process is difficult to understand
- Mismatch in real function of the app and marketing communication
- Expectation mismatch in terms of free and paid features

(Root cause: Users acquired through ad channels where marketing materials set
an expectation about the app. There is a mismatch between the result of the
onboarding process and what was promised in the marketing.)

### Proposed Solution

- Make signup process visual so it's easy to understand
- Effectively communicate app USPs during onboarding
- Build trust during onboarding process with social proof
- Create quick wins so the user can see results ASAP
- Fix any UX issues at a screen-level
- Add ability to talk to support team if they have issues
- Identify mismatch in communication vs reality across all marketing
  touchpoints

### Usability Testing — did the new design work?

- Users very easily understood what the app does and what functionality it
  offers.
- Users did not have confusions in the app; they felt the app was easily
  understood and straightforward.
- Users were motivated to use the app because it allowed them to create their
  business website quickly and easily.
- They were also interested in social media marketing, selling their
  products/services online, etc.
- Users were convinced that the Boost app can help them in their business.
- If given the option, most users completely skipped the non-mandatory
  onboarding screens.
- Almost all users were pleasantly surprised that they were able to create
  their business website easily and quickly.
- The entire experience was seamless and user friendly — just a few simple
  steps without much effort and no technical knowledge required.

### Business Impact

- **60%** decrease in app uninstall rate
- Significant decrease in average time taken for signup

## Case Study 3 — Wikipedia: KaiOS User Research

- **Agency:** Hureo
- **Year:** 2020
- **Role:** UX Research, User Personas, User Journey Mapping
- **Collaborated with:** UX Researchers, Founder

### About The Study

The Wikimedia Foundation was interested in learning about the users of
JioPhones — a smart feature phone run on KaiOS. The study was conducted to
understand these users, their phone and internet usage, Wikipedia awareness and
their motivations to use a JioPhone instead of a smartphone.

### Methodology & Participant Profile

- **Method:** One-on-one, in-person interviews. In the first 30–45 minutes,
  participants were interviewed about their background, reading behaviour,
  phone, internet, app usage and Wikipedia awareness. After the interview, we
  observed the participants' phone, app and Wikipedia usage on their devices
  through a series of tasks.
- **Duration:** Each session lasted 45–60 minutes
- **No. of participants:** 25
- **Location:** Pune, Mumbai, Delhi
- **Age & Gender:** Men and women between 18–40 years
- **Education:** College graduates, undergraduate students, and high school
  (12th grade) graduates
- **Profession:** Students, businessmen, homemakers, bank employees, salesmen,
  and field executives
- **Device Usage:** All participants were using JioPhones; a few participants
  also owned a smartphone
- **Language:** Participants could read, write and speak in Hindi, basic
  English and one or more regional languages

### What Is The Motivation To Use JioPhone?

- **Smart features at affordable price** — Users bought JioPhone instead of a
  smartphone because it had features of a smartphone at an affordable price.
- **Family members use Jio sim card** — Users bought a JioPhone to gain
  benefits of Jio cellular network as their family members also used Jio sim
  cards.
- **Easy & cheap to maintain** — Users prefer JioPhone because it is quite
  cheap to maintain and repair whereas a smartphone is expensive to get
  repaired.
- **Durable phone** — Users choose the JioPhone because it is quite durable so
  it doesn't get damaged easily.
- **Familiar with keypad** — Users prefer a keypad over a touchscreen as they
  are used to the keypad.
- **Good battery life** — Users also prefer the JioPhone because it has very
  good battery life.

### Wikipedia Feedback And Wishlist

- **More images and videos** — Users wished to see information in video format
  & more relevant images since it keeps them more interested in reading the
  article.
- **English numerals** — Users wanted to see dates & numerals in any
  Hindi/regional article to be in English numerals, as it would be easier to
  understand.
- **Index / table of contents** — Users wished to have a table of content for
  articles in the beginning so that they can jump to relevant topics.
- **Search options** — Users wished that there should be a search bar inside
  the article so that they don't have to go back to search anything new.
- **Latest information at the top** — Users wanted that once they opened a
  Wikipedia page the latest information should be shown first instead of the
  oldest.
- **UI elements** — Users wished to have a better user interface by including
  more colours, and bigger font sizes.
- **News section** — Users wished to have a section which has all the latest
  news presented from around the globe.
- **Easy Hindi words** — Users wanted Hindi articles to have simpler words for
  better understanding.
- **Copy text from articles** — Users wished to be able to copy text from
  Wikipedia articles in their Jio Phones.

### Recommendations For Wikipedia

- **Voice user interface** — Users are using voice based interaction with voice
  based Google searches. They struggle using the keyboard and search for
  specific keywords through text.
- **Optimize for readability** — Users didn't want to read in-depth information
  in paragraphs. Too much information in Wikipedia articles discouraged them
  from reading an entire article due to the small screen.
- **Optimize the UI for JioPhone** — Users have issues with the interface of
  Wikipedia articles on JioPhone like font size of content, table size, image
  quality etc.
- **Content in video format** — Users consumed information through videos
  rather than reading content online. They faced difficulty in consuming text
  heavy content.
- **Visibility for search** — Users were not aware that they could search
  articles within Wikipedia.
- **SEO for regional languages** — Users were not aware that Wikipedia
  contained information in regional languages also.
