House rule №1: no jargon

Most designer portfolios promise to synergize aesthetics with functionality. This one doesn't. I hate jargon. So, for the record, I do not:

Things I do not do: foster innovation through synergizing aesthetics and functionality · navigate the confluence of form and function · orchestrate design revolutions · instigate paradigmatic evolutions · seamlessly integrate cutting-edge methodologies · engineer experiential marvels · champion transformative ideation in the ever-evolving design ethos · perpetuate narratives that transcend boundaries

Here's what I actually do: I listen to users. I sketch. I test. I simplify. I ship. Then I check if it worked. That's the whole trick.

How a turn works

Six moves, always in this order. The order matters more than the labels.

Listen

Users, founders, analytics, support tickets. Most of what I need already exists inside the company. It just isn't in one place yet.

Map

The full journey, end to end, until I can see where it breaks.

Explore

Go wide before narrowing. I would rather throw away ten directions than defend the first one.

Refine

Pick a direction and sweat the details.

Ship

Stay with engineering through the build, screen by screen, until it's live.

Check

Watch the numbers afterwards. Fix what moved, and fix what didn't.

I read the rulebook.

The rulebook here being the codebase. I have a computer engineering degree. For years I barely used it, and it has turned out to be one of the more useful things I own.

It means I know what's expensive to build before I draw it, I can read the code instead of guessing, and engineers don't have to translate for me. When a handoff goes sideways, I can usually work out why on my own.

I'm re-learning to code properly now, and I build with AI most days. This site is where that started: the same brief went to 16 AI models, 80 directions came back, and I went through all of them to work out what I actually wanted. Useful exercise. The design decisions are still mine.

What ships with every handoff
  • Flows, states, edge cases: drawn, not implied
  • Empty, loading, error: designed up front
  • Copy: final, no lorem ipsum
  • Expensive ideas: flagged before the sprint, not discovered inside it
  • Follow-through: screen by screen until it's live

Player bio

I'm a self-taught designer with a computer engineering degree. Halfway through the degree I worked out that I liked designing products more than writing code, so I taught myself design properly: books, courses, YouTube, and a lot of bad early work.

It started in 2017 with graphic design. In 2019 I found UX research and never looked back. Since then: field research, usability testing, web apps, mobile apps, websites, brands, packaging. Print and pixels and most things in between.

I'm a curious generalist, and I think that's the useful part. Range lets me see the whole problem rather than only my corner of it.

The four players

Every color on the board stands for one of the four things I design by.

Green plays the streak

Consistency beats talent. I'm not the most talented designer in the world. I show up every day instead, and that compounds. Since 2017, 15+ clients, no year off.

Blue plays curiosity

Curiosity is the job. I know a little about a lot, which connects things other people keep separate. Understanding why someone picks a keypad phone shaped the Wikipedia work more than anything I knew about interfaces.

Gold plays outcomes

Form follows function. Function solves the problem; beauty makes people remember it, and you earn the second by doing the first. No polish moves a number until research finds the real problem.

Red plays give-a-damn

Founder's mindset, full ownership. At Turns that meant brand guidelines and a design system so the team could keep shipping after I left. Nobody asked for that part.
