TOL-02

Spec & tolerances

Four things I design by
TOL 002A◈ ±∞

Consistency beats talent

I'm not the most talented designer in the world. I show up every day instead, and that compounds. Designing since 2017, in UX since 2019, 15+ clients, no year off.

TOLERANCE: NONE — APPLIES DAILY
TOL 002B◈ ±∞

Curiosity is the job

I know a little about a lot, which helps me connect things other people keep separate. The Wikipedia study is the clearest case: understanding why someone picks a keypad phone reshaped the recommendations far more than any UI knowledge.

TOLERANCE: WIDE — KEEP ASKING
TOL 002C◈ ±∞

Form follows function

Function solves the problem. Beauty is what makes people remember it, and you earn the second by doing the first. On Boost360, no polish would have moved the number until research found the real problem.

TOLERANCE: FUNCTION FIRST
TOL 002D◈ ±∞

Give a damn

I work with a founder's mindset and take ownership of what I touch. At Turns that meant brand guidelines and a design system so the team could keep shipping after I left. Nobody asked for that part.

TOLERANCE: OWN EVERY PART
◈ MODIFICATION NOTE — MOD-042

A designer who codes

I have a computer engineering degree. For years I barely used it, and it has turned out to be one of the more useful things I own.

It means I know what's expensive to build before I draw it, I can read the codebase instead of guessing, and engineers don't have to translate for me.

I'm re-learning to code properly now, and I build with AI most days. This very page started that way: I gave one brief to 16 AI models, got 80 redesign directions back, and went through all of them to work out what I actually wanted. The design decisions are still mine.

CORRECTION · C–1 · ABOUT

Self-taught, with an engineering degree I now actually use.

Halfway through the degree I worked out I liked designing products more than writing code, so I taught myself design properly: books, courses, YouTube, and a lot of bad early work.

It started in 2017 with graphic design. Logos, posters, a board game, a kids' eBook. In 2019 I found UX research and never looked back. Since then: field research, usability testing, web apps, mobile apps, websites, brands, packaging.

I'm a curious generalist, and I think that's the useful part. Range lets me see the whole problem rather than only my corner of it.

Right now: independent product & UX design, working with founders who care. Living in Jaipur with Jerry. Designing since 2017.
