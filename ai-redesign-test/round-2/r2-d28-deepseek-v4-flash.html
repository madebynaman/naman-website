CLAUSE 4

The Method

Six steps. The order matters more than the labels. In each engagement the Designer proceeds as follows:

  1. 4.1

    Listen.

    Users, founders, analytics, support tickets. Most of what the Designer needs already exists inside the company. It just isn't in one place yet.

  2. 4.2

    Map.

    The full journey, end to end, until the point of failure is visible.

  3. 4.3

    Explore.

    Go wide before narrowing. The Designer would rather throw away ten directions than defend the first one.

  4. 4.4

    Refine.

    Pick a direction and sweat the details.

  5. 4.5

    Ship.

    Stay with engineering through the build, screen by screen, until it is live.

  6. 4.6

    Check.

    Watch the numbers afterwards. Fix what moved, and fix what didn't.

CLAUSE 5

General provisions

5.1

Consistency beats talent

I'm not the most talented designer in the world. I show up every day instead, and that compounds. Designing since 2017, in UX since 2019, 15+ clients, no year off.

5.2

Curiosity is the job

I know a little about a lot, which helps me connect things other people keep separate. The Wikipedia study is the clearest case: understanding why someone picks a keypad phone shaped the recommendations far more than any interface knowledge.

5.3

Form follows function

Function solves the problem. Beauty is what makes people remember it, and you earn the second by doing the first. On Boost360, no visual polish would have moved the number until research found the real problem.

5.4

Give a damn

I work with a founder's mindset and take ownership of what I touch. At Turns that meant brand guidelines and a design system so the team could keep shipping after I left. Nobody asked for that part.

CLAUSE 6

Consideration & capacity

A designer who codes. The Designer holds a computer engineering degree and, for years, barely used it. It has turned out to be one of the more useful things he owns.

It means he knows what is expensive to build before he draws it, he can read the codebase instead of guessing, and engineers do not have to translate for him. When a handoff goes sideways, he can usually work out why on his own.

He is re-learning to code properly now, and builds with AI most days. This page started that way: one brief, 16 AI models, 80 redesign directions, and one person deciding. The design decisions are still his.
