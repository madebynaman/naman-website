The lead essay · Designing the legible thing

I make complex products simple. A working theory on what design is for, written from the studio in Jaipur.

I'm Naman Sharma. A product and UX designer working from Jaipur. I help teams turn messy, half-formed problems into products people actually understand — and then I help engineering ship them.

Designing since 2017, in UX since 2019, agency-trained at Thence and research-trained at Hureo. Fifteen plus clients, four years running, no year off.

Field research · Wikipedia × KaiOS · 2020

Knowledge on a feature phone.

Twenty-five in-person sessions across Pune, Mumbai and Delhi with JioPhone users — for many of them the only internet-connected device in the house. The research surfaced concrete needs the Wikipedia team hadn't seen: voice input, English numerals, a table of contents, video content, and search they could not miss.

03

How the work gets made

Six steps. The order matters more than the labels.
P. 18
Method
i
Listen
Users, founders, analytics, support tickets. Most of what I need already exists inside the company. It just isn't in one place yet.
ii
Map
The full journey, end to end, until I can see where it breaks. Personas when they're honest. Maps when they're load-bearing.
iii
Explore
Go wide before narrowing. I'd rather throw away ten directions than defend the first one. Sketches on paper, options in Figma, prototypes in code.
iv
Refine
Pick a direction and sweat the details. Type, spacing, motion, system. The product earns its finish by doing the job first.
v
Ship
Stay with engineering through the build, screen by screen, until it's live. Read the codebase when it helps. Translate when it doesn't.
vi
Check
Watch the numbers afterwards. Fix what moved. Fix what didn't. The job is not done because the build is live.
04

Four things I design by

Beliefs, not billboards
P. 22
Beliefs
i

Consistency beats talent.

I'm not the most talented designer in the room. I show up every day instead, and that compounds. Designing since 2017, in UX since 2019, fifteen plus clients, no year off.

ii

Let curiosity guide you.

I know a little about a lot, which helps me connect things other people keep separate. The Wikipedia study shaped its recommendations because we understood why someone picks a keypad phone over a smartphone.

iii

Form follows function.

Function solves the problem. Beauty is what makes people remember it, and you earn the second by doing the first. On the Boost360 onboarding, no visual polish would have moved the number until research found the real problem.

iv

Give a damn.

Founder's mindset. Take ownership past the assigned frame. At Turns that meant handing the team brand guidelines and a design system so they could keep shipping consistently after I left. Nobody asked for it.

The longer version

A working life, in notes
P. 24
Profile

Designer, generalist, dog person.

Self-taught designer with a computer engineering degree. Halfway through the degree I worked out I liked designing products more than writing code, so I taught myself properly: books, courses, YouTube, and a lot of bad early work.

It started in 2017 with graphic design — logos, posters, a board game, a kids' eBook. In 2019 I found UX research and never looked back. Field research, usability testing, web apps, mobile apps, brands, packaging, social media. Print and pixels and most things in between.

I'm a curious generalist, and I think that's the useful part. Range lets me see the whole problem rather than only my corner of it.
