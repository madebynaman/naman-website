Research: Wikipedia and KaiOS

In 2020 the Wikimedia Foundation wanted to understand people who use JioPhones: cheap, durable, keypad-driven smart feature phones. Sharma, then at Hureo, ran 25 one-on-one sessions across Pune, Mumbai and Delhi, 45 to 60 minutes each, with students, homemakers, bank employees, salesmen and field executives aged 18 to 40.[4]

Participants read in Hindi, basic English and regional languages. They asked Wikipedia for more images, shorter articles, English numerals in Hindi, and a way to copy text. The team recommended voice search and voice navigation above all, because typing on a keypad was the real barrier. Findings went back as personas, journey maps and a full report.

Most of the internet is designed for people like me, on devices like mine. This study changed how I think about "the user."

How he works

Six steps, in an order he considers more important than the labels.[2]

  • Listen. Users, founders, analytics, support tickets. Most of what he needs already exists inside the company.
  • Map. The full journey, end to end, until he can see where it breaks.
  • Explore. Go wide before narrowing. He would rather throw away ten directions than defend the first one.
  • Refine. Pick a direction and sweat the details.
  • Ship. Stay with engineering through the build, screen by screen.
  • Check. Watch the numbers afterwards. Fix what moved, and fix what didn't.

Principles

  • Consistency beats talent. Designing since 2017, in UX since 2019, 15+ clients, no year off.[1]
  • Curiosity is the job. Understanding why someone picks a keypad phone over a smartphone shaped the research recommendations more than any interface knowledge.
  • Form follows function. Function solves the problem. Beauty is what makes people remember it.
  • Give a damn. At Turns he set up brand guidelines and a design system nobody asked for, so the team could keep shipping after he left.[3]
